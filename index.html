PALAWAN, PHILIPPINES

El Nido, One of a kind

Tucked at the northern tip of Palawan, El Nido is the gateway to the Bacuit Archipelago — a scattering of limestone islands, hidden lagoons, and powder-white beaches that has made it one of the most celebrated destinations in the Philippines and Southeast Asia. OVERVIEW

A bay with more history in its rock than in its records.

90,321

The total size of El Nido's managed resource area, a mix of protected land and sea spanning limestone cliffs, coral reefs, mangroves, and coastal forest.

The Nest

In year 1954, El Nido got its name that year, Spanish for "the nest," a nod to the swiftlet nests tucked into its limestone cliffs.

UNESCO

In year 1990, Palawan was declared a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve, recognizing its rare mix of marine and forest ecosystems.

270M+

The oldest rock formations in "Bacuit Bay" date back over 270 million years, among the oldest in the Philippines.

Islands & Tourist Attractions

Explore the breathtaking lagoons, pristine white sand beaches, and unique sandbars of El Nido's Bacuit Archipelago.

Big Lagoon

"Discover the beauty of Big Lagoon emerald waters, towering cliffs, and pure tranquility in El Nido."

Nestled within the heart of El Nido’s breathtaking Bacuit Bay, the Big Lagoon is one of the most iconic destinations in the Philippines. Known for its emerald waters, towering limestone cliffs, and serene ambiance, this natural wonder is a must-visit for travelers seeking adventure and tranquility.

Small Lagoon

"Discover the hidden beauty of Small Lagoon, where caves and turquoise waters await."

The Small Lagoon is a beautiful little inlet with some incredible cliffs, caves and cliff-jumping spots to explore. It’s a breathtaking natural wonder known for its serene turquoise waters and towering limestone cliffs.

Nacpan Beach

"Nacpan Beach is a tropical paradise of white sand, coconut trees, and turquoise seas."

Nacpan Beach is often hailed as one of the best beaches in the Philippines and for good reason. It offers 4 kilometers of powdery white sand, fringed by swaying coconut trees and set against the backdrop of gentle, turquoise waters.

Seven Commandos Beach

"A tropical paradise with white sand, turquoise waters, and swaying coconut trees. A perfect place to relax, swim, and enjoy."

Seven Commandos Beach, located on the main island of El Nido, Palawan, is one of the most popular destinations for tourists exploring Bacuit Bay. Known for its pristine white sand, turquoise waters, and laid-back atmosphere, the beach offers both natural beauty and convenience.

Snake Island

"Is famous for its unique winding sandbar that appears during low tide. Surrounded by clear turquoise waters..."

Snake Island is one of the most unique spots in El Nido as it connects the mainland to an island at low tide while at low tide it is a sandbar two feet under water. Officially known as Vigan Island, Snake Island has become one of the top locations to visit in El Nido.

Hidden Beach

"A peaceful white-sand cove surrounded by majestic limestone cliffs. Its clear waters are perfect for swimming and snorkeling."

A white-sand cove tucked behind El Nido’s limestone cliffs, Hidden Beach is reached through a narrow gap. Clear, calm water is ideal for a quick swim and snorkeling—go early to beat the boat crowds.

Plan your visit

Before you pack the bag or book the flight.

A quick list worth five minutes of your time now, so the rest of the trip doesn't come with any surprises.

Come Prepared

Cash

Most tour operators, tricycles, and small shops in El Nido run on cash only. Bring enough Philippine pesos to cover your stay — ATMs in town are limited and can run out, especially on weekends and holidays.

  • Nov–May (dry season): the sweet spot — calm seas, clear water, tours running as scheduled.
  • Dec–Feb: peak season, cooler weather, but busiest and priciest — book early.
  • Mar–May: hot but quieter, still solid weather.
  • Jun–Nov: monsoon season — cheaper, but expect rain and possible tour cancellations. Only recommended for flexible travelers.

  • Tours run on 4 standardized routes — Tour A, B, C, D — each covering different lagoons, beaches, and snorkeling spots.
  • Full-day tours, roughly 9 a.m.–5 p.m., lunch included.
  • Book directly through a tour operator in town or through your accommodation, ideally a day ahead.
  • Bring cash for extras (kayak rental, entrance fees not included in the tour price).

  • El Nido Town: most convenient — walk to restaurants, tour offices, and the pier.
  • Corong-Corong: quieter beachfront, short tricycle ride from town.
  • Lio: upscale, resort-style area.
  • Nacpan: remote and low-key, best for travelers who want to escape the crowds.

  • Reef-safe sunscreen
  • Reef shoes or grippy sandals
  • A dry bag for phone, cash, and camera on boat tours
  • Cash in Philippine pesos
  • Light, quick-dry clothing

  • Wear a life vest on every boat tour, even strong swimmers.
  • Book only licensed, reputable tour operators.
  • Follow your boat crew's instructions on currents and swimming zones.
  • Check weather advisories — tours can be rescheduled in rough conditions.
